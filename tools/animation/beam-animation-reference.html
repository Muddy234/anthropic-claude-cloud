Ability Animation Reference: beam abilities

Complete implementation guide for beam-type abilities — covering life_drain (static tether) and void_beam (sustained tracking beam). These abilities introduce a new channel phase and beam rendering system not present in ground_slam or melee_swing.

0 What are beam abilities?

Beam abilities connect two points with a continuous visual — a line from the caster to a target point. Unlike ground_slam (radial area) or melee_swing (directional arc), beams are linear and can persist over time. This reference covers two beam variants that share the same rendering system but behave very differently:

life_drain — Static Tether

Used by: Shade Leech (primary), Hollow Wraith (secondary)

Shape: Thin beam (0.3 tiles wide), 4 tile range

Damage: 12–18 base (scaled by tier)

Telegraph: 600ms

Channel: ~200ms (brief resolve, not sustained)

Cooldown: 4000ms

Special: Heals caster for 50% of damage dealt

Tracking: None — direction locks at telegraph start

The player experience: A pulsing dashed line flickers between the enemy and your position. You have 600ms to sidestep. If it connects, green energy particles stream from you toward the enemy — you take damage and the enemy heals. The beam is thin and short-ranged, so staying mobile keeps you safe.

void_beam — Sustained Tracking Beam

Used by: Void Sentinel (primary), Abyssal Mage (signature)

Shape: Wide beam (1 tile wide), 15 tile range

Damage: 8 per tick (every 400ms during channel)

Telegraph: 1500ms

Channel: 2000ms sustained (the beam stays active)

Cooldown: 8000ms

Special: Slow tracking — beam rotates up to 30°/sec toward player

Tracking: Slow rotation during channel phase

The player experience: A wide purple line charges up from the enemy, growing brighter over 1.5 seconds. Then a massive beam fires and stays active for 2 seconds, slowly sweeping to follow you. You can't just sidestep once — you need to keep circling. The beam deals damage every tick you're inside it. Dark particles spray at the beam's end point and the screen shakes for the full duration.

Why two abilities in one reference? life_drain and void_beam share the same underlying beam rendering system — a line between two points with configurable width, particles, and tracking. Building the renderer for one gives you the other nearly for free. The difference is in the data: width, duration, tracking speed, particle direction, and feedback effects. This reference shows how one system serves both.

1 The Animation Lifecycle

Beam abilities introduce a new phase that ground_slam and melee_swing don't have: the channel phase. For life_drain, the channel is a brief visual-only resolve (~200ms). For void_beam, the channel is a sustained 2-second window where the beam is active and dealing damage continuously.

TELEGRAPH The beam charges up

  • life_drain (600ms): A dashed pulsing line appears from the caster toward the target's captured position. The dashes shimmer in/out. Thin and subtle — easy to miss if you're not watching.
  • void_beam (1500ms): A solid line grows in intensity from the caster toward max range. Starts dim, grows brighter and wider. The charging effect makes it obvious something devastating is coming.
  • Both types lock direction when the telegraph starts (life_drain is permanent, void_beam allows slow tracking once the channel begins)
  • The telegraph line extends from the caster's center to either the target point (life_drain) or max range (void_beam)

CHANNEL The beam fires / persists

  • life_drain (~200ms): The beam briefly solidifies — bright core with glow. Energy particles flow from the hit point back to the caster. Caster pulses green (heal feedback). Then it fades. Damage is dealt once, instantly.
  • void_beam (2000ms): The beam stays active for 2 full seconds. A bright inner core with wide outer glow. Slowly rotates toward the player (30°/sec max). Damage ticks every 400ms to anyone inside the beam. Particles spray at the beam terminus. Screen shakes continuously.
  • This phase is where beam abilities differ most from ground_slam — the effect persists instead of resolving instantly

RECOVERY The punish window

  • life_drain (500ms): The caster staggers slightly, spent from channeling. Takes 1.15x damage. The player can rush in for a punish, but the window is moderate.
  • void_beam (800ms): The caster is exhausted — hunched, visibly drained. Takes 1.35x damage (the highest punish multiplier of any ability). This rewards the player for surviving the full beam.
  • Both enemies are immobile and cannot start new abilities during recovery

IDLE Cooldown period

  • life_drain (~2700ms remaining): Moderate cooldown — the enemy uses this often to sustain itself. Expect it every 4 seconds.
  • void_beam (~3700ms remaining): Long cooldown — this is a signature ability, not a spammable attack. The player gets a long breather between beams.
  • Both enemies return to normal AI behavior, repositioning and using other abilities while the cooldown ticks

3 Sprite Frames (What to Draw)

Beam abilities have different visual components than area or melee attacks. Instead of shapes on the floor, you're drawing lines connecting two points with effects layered on top.

A. Telegraph Frames (beam indicator — drawn below entities)

For life_drain: a pulsing dashed line. For void_beam: a solid line growing in intensity. Both extend from the caster toward the target.

B. Channel Frames (active beam — drawn above entities)

For life_drain: a brief bright tether with a core glow. For void_beam: a sustained wide beam with inner core and outer glow. The beam connects caster to target/terminus.

C. Particle Frames (energy flow / terminus spray)

For life_drain: small green orbs flowing FROM target TO caster (reverse). For void_beam: dark purple sparks spraying outward at the beam's end point.

D. Caster Feedback (life_drain only — heal pulse)

A green ring expanding outward from the caster when drain damage connects. Brief flash, 300ms duration.

4 JavaScript Implementation

Beam abilities need three new things the existing system doesn't have: beam rendering (drawing a line between two points), a channel phase (the effect persists and deals damage over time), and beam hit detection (checking if a player is inside a rectangle, not a circle or arc).

Part A — The Data: Define what each beam ability looks like

These go in effect-sprites.js alongside ground_slam and melee_swing entries.

The key architectural addition: The channel block is new. ground_slam and melee_swing have telegraph → instant resolve → recovery. Beam abilities have telegraph → channel (sustained) → recovery. The channel phase is where the beam is visually active and dealing damage. For life_drain, the channel is short (200ms, effectively instant). For void_beam, the channel is the main event — 2 seconds of sustained damage and tracking.

Part B — The Controller: New channel phase and beam tracking

The EffectAnimator needs a 'channel' phase and logic for beam tracking (rotating toward the player over time).

// ─── effect-animator.js (additions for beam abilities) ───

startEffect(abilityId, x, y, telegraphMs, radius, direction) {
  // ... existing setup (same as ground_slam / melee_swing) ...

  const config = ABILITY_EFFECTS[abilityId];

  this.activeEffects.set(id, {
    // ... existing fields ...
    id, abilityId, config, x, y, radius, direction,
    phase: 'telegraph',
    phaseTimer: 0,
    telegraphDuration: telegraphMs,
    particles: [],

    // ════ NEW: Beam-specific state ════
    beamDirection: direction || 0,   // Current beam angle (may update during channel)
    beamEndX: 0,                     // Computed end point (world coords)
    beamEndY: 0,                     // Updated every frame for tracking beams
    casterX: x,                     // Caster position (may update if caster moves)
    casterY: y,
    halfWidth: 0,                    // Half beam width in world units (for hit detection)
    channelTickTimer: 0,             // Tracks time since last damage tick
    particleSpawnTimer: 0,           // Tracks time since last particle spawn
  });

  // Compute initial beam end point
  this.updateBeamEndPoint(this.activeEffects.get(id));

  return id;
},

/**
 * Update loop — modified to handle the 'channel' phase.
 */
update(deltaTime) {
  for (const [id, effect] of this.activeEffects) {
    effect.phaseTimer += deltaTime;

    switch (effect.phase) {

      case 'telegraph':
        // Same as before — wait for resolve() call from ability system
        break;

      case 'channel':  // ════ NEW PHASE ════
        const chCfg = effect.config.channel;

        // ── Tracking: slowly rotate beam toward current target ──
        if (chCfg.trackingSpeed > 0) {
          this.updateBeamTracking(effect, deltaTime);
        }

        // ── Update beam end point ──
        this.updateBeamEndPoint(effect);

        // ── Spawn particles continuously ──
        effect.particleSpawnTimer += deltaTime;
        const spawnRate = effect.config.particles?.spawnRate || 100;
        while (effect.particleSpawnTimer >= spawnRate) {
          this.spawnBeamParticles(effect);
          effect.particleSpawnTimer -= spawnRate;
        }

        // ── Update existing particles ──
        this.updateParticles(effect, deltaTime);

        // ── Damage ticks (for sustained beams) ──
        if (chCfg.tickInterval) {
          effect.channelTickTimer += deltaTime;
          if (effect.channelTickTimer >= chCfg.tickInterval) {
            effect.channelTickTimer -= chCfg.tickInterval;
            // Emit a 'beam_tick' event for the ability system to handle
            EventBus.emit('beam_tick', { effectId: id, effect });
          }
        }

        // ── End channel when duration expires ──
        if (effect.phaseTimer >= chCfg.duration) {
          effect.phase = 'fading';
          effect.phaseTimer = 0;
        }
        break;

      case 'fading':   // Brief fade-out after channel ends
        this.updateParticles(effect, deltaTime);
        if (effect.phaseTimer >= 300 && effect.particles.every(p => p.life <= 0)) {
          effect.phase = 'done';
        }
        break;

      case 'impact':  // Existing (for non-beam abilities)
        // ... unchanged ...
        break;

      case 'done':
        this.activeEffects.delete(id);
        break;
    }
  }
},

/**
 * Resolve for beam abilities — transitions telegraph → channel.
 * For life_drain: deals instant damage, starts brief channel visual.
 * For void_beam: starts sustained channel with tracking.
 */
resolve(effectId) {
  const effect = this.activeEffects.get(effectId);
  if (!effect) return;

  // If this ability has a channel phase, go there instead of 'impact'
  if (effect.config.channel) {
    effect.phase = 'channel';
    effect.phaseTimer = 0;
    effect.channelTickTimer = 0;
    effect.particleSpawnTimer = 0;
    effect.halfWidth = (effect.config.channel.width / 2) * TILE_SIZE;

    // Start sustained camera effects
    if (effect.config.camera?.duringChannel) {
      Camera.startSustainedShake(
        effect.config.camera.duringChannel.intensity,
        effect.config.channel.duration
      );
    }

    // Play channel audio (may loop)
    if (effect.config.audio?.channel) {
      AudioManager.play(effect.config.audio.channel, { loop: true });
    }

    // Caster feedback (life_drain heal pulse)
    if (effect.config.casterFeedback) {
      this.startCasterFeedback(effect);
    }
  } else {
    // Non-beam abilities: existing resolve behavior
    effect.phase = 'impact';
    effect.phaseTimer = 0;
    this.spawnParticles(effect);
  }
},

// ── Beam tracking: rotate toward current target ──

updateBeamTracking(effect, dt) {
  const trackSpeed = effect.config.channel.trackingSpeed;  // degrees/sec
  const maxRotation = (trackSpeed * Math.PI / 180) * (dt / 1000);  // radians this frame

  // Get current target position from ability system
  const target = EnemyAbilitySystem.getCurrentTarget(effect.abilityId);
  if (!target) return;

  // Desired angle toward target
  const desiredAngle = Math.atan2(
    target.worldY - effect.casterY,
    target.worldX - effect.casterX
  );

  // Compute shortest angular difference
  let angleDiff = desiredAngle - effect.beamDirection;

  // Normalize to [-π, π]
  while (angleDiff > Math.PI) angleDiff -= Math.PI * 2;
  while (angleDiff < -Math.PI) angleDiff += Math.PI * 2;

  // Clamp rotation to max speed
  const rotation = Math.sign(angleDiff) * Math.min(Math.abs(angleDiff), maxRotation);
  effect.beamDirection += rotation;
},

// ── Compute beam end point from direction + range ──

updateBeamEndPoint(effect) {
  const range = (effect.config.telegraph?.maxRange || effect.radius) * TILE_SIZE;
  effect.beamEndX = effect.casterX + Math.cos(effect.beamDirection) * range;
  effect.beamEndY = effect.casterY + Math.sin(effect.beamDirection) * range;
},

// ── Spawn beam particles (along beam or at terminus) ──

spawnBeamParticles(effect) {
  const cfg = effect.config.particles;
  if (!cfg) return;

  if (cfg.spawnMode === 'along_beam') {
    // Spawn along the beam line, particles flow toward caster
    const t = 0.3 + Math.random() * 0.7;  // Bias toward target end
    const bx = effect.casterX + (effect.beamEndX - effect.casterX) * t;
    const by = effect.casterY + (effect.beamEndY - effect.casterY) * t;

    // Perpendicular sway
    const perpAngle = effect.beamDirection + Math.PI / 2;
    const sway = (Math.random() - 0.5) * 2 * (cfg.sway || 0);

    // Velocity: toward caster (reverse flow)
    const towardCaster = effect.beamDirection + Math.PI;
    const speed = cfg.flowSpeed || 100;

    effect.particles.push({
      x: bx + Math.cos(perpAngle) * sway,
      y: by + Math.sin(perpAngle) * sway,
      vx: Math.cos(towardCaster) * speed,
      vy: Math.sin(towardCaster) * speed,
      life: cfg.lifetime,
      maxLife: cfg.lifetime,
      sprite: cfg.sprite,
    });
  }

  else if (cfg.spawnMode === 'beam_terminus') {
    // Spawn at the far end, spray outward
    for (let i = 0; i < (cfg.count || 3); i++) {
      const angle = effect.beamDirection + (Math.random() - 0.5) * Math.PI;
      const radial = cfg.velocity.radialRange[0]
        + Math.random() * (cfg.velocity.radialRange[1] - cfg.velocity.radialRange[0]);
      const tangent = cfg.velocity.tangentialRange[0]
        + Math.random() * (cfg.velocity.tangentialRange[1] - cfg.velocity.tangentialRange[0]);

      effect.particles.push({
        x: effect.beamEndX + (Math.random() - 0.5) * 6,
        y: effect.beamEndY + (Math.random() - 0.5) * 6,
        vx: Math.cos(angle) * radial + Math.cos(angle + Math.PI/2) * tangent,
        vy: Math.sin(angle) * radial + Math.sin(angle + Math.PI/2) * tangent,
        life: cfg.lifetime,
        maxLife: cfg.lifetime,
        sprite: cfg.sprite,
      });
    }
  }
},

// ── Caster feedback (heal pulse ring) ──

startCasterFeedback(effect) {
  const fb = effect.config.casterFeedback;
  effect.feedbackTimer = 0;
  effect.feedbackDuration = fb.duration;
  effect.feedbackActive = true;
}

Part C — The Renderer: Drawing beams

The beam renderer draws a line between two world positions with configurable width, core/glow layers, and dashed/solid styles. This is called during both drawGround() (telegraphs) and drawOverlay() (active beams).

// ─── effect-renderer.js (beam rendering additions) ───

// ── TELEGRAPH: beam_telegraph type ──

drawTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.telegraph;

  // Dispatch by type (existing types + new beam type)
  if (cfg.type === 'circle_grow') { this.drawTelegraphCircle(ctx,effect,camX,camY); return; }
  if (cfg.type === 'arc_fill')     { this.drawTelegraphArc(ctx,effect,camX,camY);    return; }
  if (cfg.type === 'beam_telegraph') { this.drawBeamTelegraph(ctx,effect,camX,camY); return; }
},

drawBeamTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.telegraph;
  const progress = Math.min(effect.phaseTimer / effect.telegraphDuration, 1.0);

  // Screen coords for start (caster) and end
  const sx = effect.casterX - camX;
  const sy = effect.casterY - camY;
  const ex = effect.beamEndX - camX;
  const ey = effect.beamEndY - camY;

  // Beam width in pixels
  const widthPx = cfg.width * TILE_SIZE;

  // Opacity — static or charging
  let alpha = cfg.fillOpacity || 0.3;
  if (cfg.chargeIntensity) {
    alpha = (cfg.startOpacity || 0.1) +
            ((cfg.endOpacity || 0.5) - (cfg.startOpacity || 0.1)) * progress;
  }

  ctx.save();
  ctx.globalAlpha = alpha;

  if (cfg.style === 'dashed') {
    // ── Dashed pulsing line (life_drain) ──
    const pulse = 0.5 + 0.5 * Math.sin(effect.phaseTimer / (cfg.pulseRate || 200) * Math.PI);
    ctx.globalAlpha = alpha * (0.4 + pulse * 0.6);

    ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.color;
    ctx.lineWidth = widthPx;
    ctx.lineCap = 'round';
    ctx.setLineDash([cfg.dashLength || 8, cfg.dashGap || 6]);
    // Animate dash offset for "flowing" effect
    ctx.lineDashOffset = -effect.phaseTimer * 0.05;

    ctx.beginPath();
    ctx.moveTo(sx, sy);
    ctx.lineTo(ex, ey);
    ctx.stroke();

    ctx.setLineDash([]);  // Reset
  } else {
    // ── Solid charging line (void_beam) ──
    // Outer glow
    ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.color;
    ctx.lineWidth = widthPx + 4;
    ctx.lineCap = 'round';
    ctx.globalAlpha = alpha * 0.3;
    ctx.beginPath();
    ctx.moveTo(sx, sy);
    ctx.lineTo(ex, ey);
    ctx.stroke();

    // Core line
    ctx.globalAlpha = alpha;
    ctx.lineWidth = widthPx;
    ctx.beginPath();
    ctx.moveTo(sx, sy);
    ctx.lineTo(ex, ey);
    ctx.stroke();
  }

  // Border edges (both styles)
  ctx.globalAlpha = alpha * 0.8;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.borderColor;
  ctx.lineWidth = 1;
  // Draw two parallel lines along beam edges
  const perpX = Math.cos(effect.beamDirection + Math.PI/2) * widthPx/2;
  const perpY = Math.sin(effect.beamDirection + Math.PI/2) * widthPx/2;
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.moveTo(sx + perpX, sy + perpY); ctx.lineTo(ex + perpX, ey + perpY);
  ctx.moveTo(sx - perpX, sy - perpY); ctx.lineTo(ex - perpX, ey - perpY);
  ctx.stroke();

  ctx.restore();
},

// ── CHANNEL: active beam rendering ──

drawOverlay(ctx, cameraX, cameraY) {
  for (const [id, effect] of EffectAnimator.activeEffects) {
    if (effect.phase === 'impact') {
      this.drawImpact(ctx, effect, cameraX, cameraY);
      this.drawParticles(ctx, effect, cameraX, cameraY);
    }
    if (effect.phase === 'channel' || effect.phase === 'fading') {    // ← NEW
      this.drawBeamChannel(ctx, effect, cameraX, cameraY);
      this.drawParticles(ctx, effect, cameraX, cameraY);
      this.drawCasterFeedback(ctx, effect, cameraX, cameraY);
    }
  }
},

drawBeamChannel(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.channel;

  const sx = effect.casterX - camX;
  const sy = effect.casterY - camY;
  const ex = effect.beamEndX - camX;
  const ey = effect.beamEndY - camY;

  // Width with optional flicker
  let widthPx = cfg.width * TILE_SIZE;
  if (cfg.flicker) {
    const flick = Math.sin(effect.phaseTimer / 1000 * cfg.flicker.speed * Math.PI * 2);
    widthPx *= (1 + flick * cfg.flicker.amount);
  }

  // Fade multiplier (beam dims during 'fading' phase)
  let fadeMult = 1.0;
  if (effect.phase === 'fading') {
    fadeMult = Math.max(0, 1.0 - effect.phaseTimer / 300);
  }

  ctx.save();

  // Layer 1: Outer glow
  if (cfg.glowRadius) {
    ctx.globalAlpha = 0.15 * fadeMult;
    ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.glowColor || cfg.color;
    ctx.lineWidth = cfg.glowRadius * TILE_SIZE * 2;
    ctx.lineCap = 'round';
    ctx.beginPath();
    ctx.moveTo(sx, sy); ctx.lineTo(ex, ey);
    ctx.stroke();
  }

  // Layer 2: Main beam body
  ctx.globalAlpha = 0.7 * fadeMult;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.color;
  ctx.lineWidth = widthPx;
  ctx.lineCap = 'round';
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.moveTo(sx, sy); ctx.lineTo(ex, ey);
  ctx.stroke();

  // Layer 3: Bright inner core
  ctx.globalAlpha = 0.9 * fadeMult;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.coreColor;
  ctx.lineWidth = (cfg.coreWidth || 0.15) * TILE_SIZE;
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.moveTo(sx, sy); ctx.lineTo(ex, ey);
  ctx.stroke();

  ctx.restore();
},

// ── CASTER FEEDBACK: expanding heal ring ──

drawCasterFeedback(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  if (!effect.feedbackActive) return;
  const fb = effect.config.casterFeedback;
  if (!fb) return;

  effect.feedbackTimer += 0;  // Timer updated in main update loop
  const progress = Math.min(effect.feedbackTimer / fb.duration, 1.0);
  if (progress >= 1.0) { effect.feedbackActive = false; return; }

  const radius = (fb.startRadius + (fb.endRadius - fb.startRadius) * progress) * TILE_SIZE;
  const alpha = progress > fb.fadeStart ? 1.0 - ((progress - fb.fadeStart) / (1.0 - fb.fadeStart)) : 1.0;

  const cx = effect.casterX - camX;
  const cy = effect.casterY - camY;

  ctx.save();
  ctx.globalAlpha = alpha * 0.7;
  ctx.strokeStyle = fb.color;
  ctx.lineWidth = fb.ringWidth;
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.arc(cx, cy, radius, 0, Math.PI * 2);
  ctx.stroke();
  ctx.restore();
}

Part D — Hit Detection: isInsideBeam()

This is the core collision check for beam abilities. Instead of a simple circle radius check, beams need a point-to-line-segment distance test. This function is called by the ability system during the channel phase to determine if a player is inside the beam.

// ─── In enemy-ability-system.js ───
// Called during beam_tick events to check if the player is inside the beam.
// Uses perpendicular distance from the player to the beam line segment.
//
// How it works:
//   1. Project the player position onto the beam line
//   2. Clamp t to [0,1] so we only check the actual beam segment
//   3. Find the nearest point on the segment
//   4. If the distance to that point is less than half the beam width, hit!

isInsideBeam(px, py, effect) {
  // Get beam line segment endpoints
  const x1 = effect.casterX;
  const y1 = effect.casterY;
  const x2 = effect.beamEndX;
  const y2 = effect.beamEndY;

  // Length of beam segment squared (avoid sqrt)
  const lineLengthSq = (x2 - x1) ** 2 + (y2 - y1) ** 2;

  // Project player position onto beam line → parameter t ∈ [0, 1]
  const t = Math.max(0, Math.min(1,
    ((px - x1) * (x2 - x1) + (py - y1) * (y2 - y1)) / lineLengthSq
  ));

  // Nearest point on the beam segment to the player
  const nearestX = x1 + t * (x2 - x1);
  const nearestY = y1 + t * (y2 - y1);

  // Distance from player to nearest point on beam
  const distance = Math.hypot(px - nearestX, py - nearestY);

  // Hit if within half the beam width
  return distance <= effect.halfWidth;
}

// ── Usage in beam_tick handler ──

EventBus.on('beam_tick', ({ effectId, effect }) => {
  // Check all players against beam
  for (const player of playerList) {
    if (this.isInsideBeam(player.worldX, player.worldY, effect)) {
      // Apply tick damage
      player.takeDamage(effect.tickDamage);
    }
  }
});
Critical — beam hit detection vs circle hit detection: For ground_slam, you check distance(player, center) < radius. For beams, you check perpendicular distance to a line segment. The t parameter clamp to [0,1] is essential — without it, a player standing behind the caster (past the start point) or far past the beam's end would still register as "inside the beam." The segment clamp ensures only the actual beam path counts.

Part E — The Glue: Connect beam abilities to the ability system

// ─── In enemy-ability-system.js ───
// Modified executeAbility() for beam-type abilities.
// The key difference: beam abilities may have a sustained channel phase
// where the enemy remains locked and the beam persists.

executeAbility(enemy, target, ability, abilitySet) {
  const telegraphTime = /* ... existing ... */;
  const recoveryTime = ability.recoveryTime || 600;
  const effectConfig = ABILITY_EFFECTS[ability.id];

  }

  );
  enemy._currentEffectId = effectId;
  if (direction !== undefined) enemy.facingAngle = direction;

  // After telegraph: resolve
  setTimeout(() => {
    // ... existing safety checks (enemy alive, not stunned) ...

    EffectAnimator.resolve(effectId);

    // For instant-resolve beams (life_drain), deal damage now
    if (!effectConfig?.channel?.tickInterval) {
      this.resolveAbility(enemy, target, ability);
    }

    // ════ NEW: For sustained beams, keep enemy locked during channel ════
    const channelDuration = effectConfig?.channel?.duration || 0;

    if (channelDuration > 500) {
      // Keep enemy in 'channeling' state for the full channel duration
      enemy.abilityState = 'channeling';

      setTimeout(() => {
        // Channel ends — stop audio, enter recovery
        if (effectConfig.audio?.channel) {
          AudioManager.stop(effectConfig.audio.channel);
        }
        if (effectConfig.audio?.channelEnd) {
          AudioManager.play(effectConfig.audio.channelEnd);
        }
        this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime, enemy.id);
      }, channelDuration);

    } else {
      // Short channel (life_drain) — go straight to recovery
      this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime, enemy.id);
    }

  }, telegraphTime);
}

6 Adapting This for Other Beam / Channel Variants

The beam system handles any ability that connects two points or persists over time. Here's how to extend it:

For lightning_bolt (instant beam, no channel):

Set channel.duration: 80 (nearly instant), trackingSpeed: 0, wide core (0.5 tiles), no glow. The visual is a bright jagged line that flashes once. Use a zigzag line style instead of straight — the renderer draws 4–6 random perpendicular offsets along the beam path to simulate a lightning bolt. Add a bright white flash frame at the start. Particles are bright white sparks spraying from the impact point.

For frost_ray (slow sustained beam, freezes on hit):

Similar to void_beam: channel.duration: 1500, trackingSpeed: 20 (slower tracking). Width 0.5 tiles. Ice-blue color scheme (#88CCFF core, #4488CC outer). Particles are snowflake-style sprites flowing along the beam. On sustained hit, apply a stacking slow debuff — each tick increases slow by 10%. Caster feedback: frost crystals growing on caster's hands (cosmetic sprite overlay).

For chain_lightning (multi-target beam):

Extension of the beam system: start with one beam from caster to primary target, then on hit, spawn additional beams from the hit target to nearby secondary targets. Each chain segment is a separate beam effect with duration: 100 and zigzag rendering. Chain targets are computed by the ability system, not the animation system — each segment gets its own startEffect() call with explicit start/end positions instead of using direction + range.

For tractor_beam (pull effect):

Beam with gameplay effect: during channel, the target is pulled toward the caster at a rate of 1 tile/sec. Visual is similar to life_drain but with a different particle direction — particles flow from caster TO target (the energy pushes outward, but the target gets dragged in). Width 0.4 tiles, blue-white color. The ability system applies a position force on the target during each update tick, not just damage.
