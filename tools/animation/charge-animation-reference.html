Ability Animation Reference: charge

Demon Knight signature ability — a line telegraph with enemy movement, stun on hit, and wall collision. This covers mechanics that ground_slam didn't: directional targeting, physical movement during resolve, and conditional outcomes.

0 What is charge?

Used by: Demon Knight (primary)

Shape: Line, width 2 tiles, length 10 tiles — aimed at the player's position when telegraph begins

Damage: 35 base (scaled by tier)

Telegraph: 800ms — a red line appears on the ground showing the exact path

Resolve: ~300ms — the Knight physically moves along the line at high speed

Recovery: 1000ms — the Knight is staggered after charging (longest punish window of any regular enemy)

Cooldown: 6000ms

Effect on hit: Stun (1 second) — the player can't act

Wall collision: If the Knight hits a wall, it takes 10-15 self-damage and is stunned for 500ms-1000ms

The player experience: You hear a roar and see a red line lock onto the ground. You have 0.8 seconds to sidestep. The Knight rockets down the line — if it hits you, you're stunned and in trouble. If you dodge, the Knight overshoots and is vulnerable for a full second. If you bait it into a wall, the Knight stuns itself. This is the most satisfying enemy to outplay in the game.

Why charge is different from ground_slam: ground_slam is stationary — the enemy doesn't move, the danger zone is a circle around it. Charge is directional and mobile — the enemy physically travels across the arena, the danger zone is a line from point A to point B, and where the enemy ends up after the charge matters. This introduces five new animation problems: drawing a line telegraph, moving the enemy sprite during resolve, handling a hit vs miss outcome, handling wall collision, and showing a stun effect on the player.

1 The Animation Lifecycle

Charge has one more sub-phase than ground_slam because the enemy moves during resolve. The movement itself is the attack — it's not instant like ground_slam's damage application.

TELEGRAPH Warning phase (800ms)

  • The Knight stops moving and locks its facing direction toward the target
  • A red rectangle appears on the ground from the Knight to 10 tiles ahead
  • The rectangle fades in over 200ms then pulses
  • The Knight plays a wind-up animation: leans back, weapon raised
  • A rising growl/roar sound builds during the windup
  • The line is locked in place — if the player moves, the line does NOT follow

The line locks on to where the player IS, not where they're going. The player must move sideways off the line before 800ms expires.

DASH Movement phase (~300ms)

  • The telegraph line disappears
  • The Knight physically moves from its start position along the line at high speed
  • A speed blur trail follows behind the Knight (3-4 afterimages that fade)
  • Dust particles kick up along the path
  • The Knight moves until it hits a player, hits a wall, or reaches the end of the line
  • Dash speed: ~33 tiles/sec (10 tiles in ~300ms)

This is the only phase where the enemy's position actually changes. The animation system must interpolate the Knight's grid position smoothly along the charge path.

RESOLVE Three possible outcomes

Outcome A — Hit player:

  • 35 damage applied to the player
  • Player is stunned for 1 second (yellow stars above head)
  • The Knight stops at the player's position
  • Impact shockwave visual (small ring burst, like a mini ground_slam)
  • Heavy impact sound

Outcome B — Hit wall:

  • The Knight takes 10-15 self-damage
  • The Knight is stunned for 500-1000ms (longer recovery)
  • Wall crack visual at impact point
  • Different crash sound (metallic clang + stone crack)
  • Screen shake (2px, 150ms)

Outcome C — Miss (reach end of line):

  • The Knight skids to a stop at the end of the charge path
  • Small dust cloud at feet
  • Enters normal recovery

RECOVERY The punish window (1000ms)

  • The Knight plays a staggered/off-balance animation — hunched forward, weapon dragging
  • Takes 1.25x damage during this window
  • Cannot move or start another ability
  • If wall collision: recovery is extended (1500-2000ms) and Knight shows "dizzy stars" visual

This is the longest punish window in the regular roster. The charge is devastating if it hits, but the Knight is extremely vulnerable afterward. The entire Demon Knight fight is built around this cycle: dodge the charge, punish the recovery, reposition before the next one.

2 Key Differences from ground_slam

If your team already built ground_slam, here's exactly what's new about charge. Everything not listed here works the same way.

Mechanicground_slamchargeWhat changes
Telegraph shape Circle (radius) Rectangle (line) New telegraph type: line_fixed. Needs start point, end point, and width instead of center + radius.
Telegraph direction Omnidirectional Aimed at target Must calculate angle from enemy to target at telegraph start, then lock it. Telegraph does NOT rotate to follow target.
Enemy movement None — stays in place Moves along charge line New resolve sub-phase: dash. Enemy position interpolates from start to end over ~300ms. Grid position must update each frame.
Damage application Instant AoE at resolve Collision check during dash Damage happens when the Knight's moving hitbox overlaps a player, not at a fixed time. Must check collision each frame of the dash.
Resolve outcome Always the same Three branches Hit player, hit wall, or miss. Each has different visual and gameplay consequences. The animation system must handle all three.
Impact visual Expanding rings from center Shockwave at collision point OR skid dust at end Impact position is wherever the Knight stops, not where the attack started. Different visuals per outcome.
Status effect None Stun (1s) on player Must show stun visual on affected player (yellow stars, frozen pose). The stun visual is a separate effect that attaches to the player entity, not the ability.
Trail visual None Speed blur afterimages 3-4 translucent copies of the Knight sprite trailing behind during dash, each progressively more transparent. Fade over 200ms after dash ends.
Self-damage None Wall collision hurts enemy If charge path hits a wall tile, Knight stops at wall, takes damage, extended recovery. Must ray-cast along charge path to find wall intersection.
Recovery duration 800ms (fixed) 1000ms normal / 1500-2000ms wall Recovery length depends on outcome. Wall collision extends vulnerability window — this is the player's reward for baiting the charge into a wall.

4 Visual Frame Breakdowns

A. Telegraph Frames (red line on ground — 800ms)

A red rectangle from the Knight to the charge target. Fades in over 200ms, then pulses for the remaining 600ms. The line is locked in place the moment it appears — it never rotates.

B. Dash Trail Frames (speed blur — ~300ms)

The Knight moves along the line. 3-4 afterimages trail behind, each more transparent. Dust particles kick up along the path.

C. Impact Frames (hit/wall/miss — plays once on contact)

Notice how the data structure grew to handle new complexity: ground_slam had telegraph + impact + particles. Charge has telegraph + dash (with trail and dust) + three impact variants + a stun visual + five audio cues. The controller and renderer need to be smart enough to handle these additional components, but the data format stays consistent — it's always "here's what this phase looks like, here are the numbers."

Part B — The Controller: Managing charge state

The EffectAnimator from ground_slam needs new logic for the dash phase and branching outcomes. Here's what changes.

// ─── In effect-animator.js ───
// New method: startChargeEffect — called instead of startEffect for line abilities

startChargeEffect(abilityId, enemy, targetX, targetY) {
  const id = this.nextId++;
  const config = ABILITY_EFFECTS[abilityId];
  if (!config) return -1;

  // ── Calculate the charge path ──
  // Direction from enemy to target, locked at this moment
  const dx = targetX - enemy.worldX;
  const dy = targetY - enemy.worldY;
  const angle = Math.atan2(dy, dx);

  // Charge endpoint: 10 tiles in this direction (or wall, whichever is first)
  const maxLength = config.telegraph.length * TILE_SIZE;
  const wallCheck = this.raycastToWall(enemy.worldX, enemy.worldY, angle, maxLength);

  const endX = wallCheck.hitWall
    ? wallCheck.wallX - Math.cos(angle) * (TILE_SIZE * 0.5)  // Stop just before wall
    : enemy.worldX + Math.cos(angle) * maxLength;

  const endY = wallCheck.hitWall
    ? wallCheck.wallY - Math.sin(angle) * (TILE_SIZE * 0.5)
    : enemy.worldY + Math.sin(angle) * maxLength;

  const actualLength = Math.hypot(endX - enemy.worldX, endY - enemy.worldY);
  const dashDuration = (actualLength / TILE_SIZE) / config.dash.speed * 1000;

  this.activeEffects.set(id, {
    id,
    abilityId,
    config,
    angle,                              // Locked direction

    // ── Path definition ──
    startX: enemy.worldX,
    startY: enemy.worldY,
    endX, endY,
    hitsWall: wallCheck.hitWall,
    actualLength,

    // ── Phase tracking ──
    phase: 'telegraph',                // 'telegraph' | 'dash' | 'impact' | 'done'
    phaseTimer: 0,
    telegraphDuration: 800,
    dashDuration,

    // ── Trail state (populated during dash) ──
    trailPositions: [],                // Array of {x, y, alpha, age}
    particles: [],
    enemy,                             // Reference to move the enemy during dash
  });

  return id;
},

// ── In the update() method, add a 'dash' case: ──

case 'dash': {
  // Interpolate enemy position along the charge path
  const dashProgress = Math.min(effect.phaseTimer / effect.dashDuration, 1.0);
  const currentX = effect.startX + (effect.endX - effect.startX) * dashProgress;
  const currentY = effect.startY + (effect.endY - effect.startY) * dashProgress;

  // Move the actual enemy entity
  effect.enemy.worldX = currentX;
  effect.enemy.worldY = currentY;

  // Record position for trail afterimages
  effect.trailPositions.push({
    x: currentX, y: currentY,
    alpha: effect.config.dash.trail.startAlpha,
    age: 0
  });

  // Trim trail to max count
  while (effect.trailPositions.length > effect.config.dash.trail.count) {
    effect.trailPositions.shift();
  }

  // ── Collision check each frame ──
  const hitPlayer = this.checkPlayerCollision(currentX, currentY, effect.config.telegraph.width);

  if (hitPlayer) {
    // HIT PLAYER — stop here, apply damage + stun
    effect.outcome = 'hitPlayer';
    effect.impactX = currentX;
    effect.impactY = currentY;
    effect.phase = 'impact';
    effect.phaseTimer = 0;
    break;
  }

  if (dashProgress >= 1.0) {
    // Reached end of path — wall or miss
    effect.outcome = effect.hitsWall ? 'hitWall' : 'miss';
    effect.impactX = effect.endX;
    effect.impactY = effect.endY;
    effect.phase = 'impact';
    effect.phaseTimer = 0;
  }

  // Update dust particles
  this.updateParticles(effect, deltaTime);
  break;
}
The enemy position changes during dash. This is the biggest architectural difference from ground_slam. During ground_slam, the enemy stays put and only the visual effect moves. During charge, the enemy entity physically relocates across the grid. This means the EnemyAI, pathfinding, and collision systems must all respect the new position after the charge ends. The effect controller writes directly to enemy.worldX/worldY — this is intentional and necessary. The abilityLockout flag prevents the AI from fighting over the position during the dash.

Part C — The Renderer: Drawing charge visuals

The renderer needs new drawing functions for line telegraphs and afterimage trails. The impact ring and particles reuse the ground_slam renderer.

// ─── In effect-renderer.js ───
// New telegraph type: line_fixed

drawTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.telegraph;

  if (cfg.type === 'circle_grow') {
    this.drawCircleTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY);  // Existing ground_slam code
  }
  else if (cfg.type === 'line_fixed') {
    this.drawLineTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY);     // New
  }
  // Future: 'cone_grow' for fire_breath, etc.
},

drawLineTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.telegraph;
  const progress = Math.min(effect.phaseTimer / effect.telegraphDuration, 1.0);

  // Fade in during first 200ms
  const fadeIn = Math.min(effect.phaseTimer / cfg.fadeInTime, 1.0);

  // Pulse after fade-in completes
  const pulse = fadeIn >= 1.0
    ? 0.7 + 0.3 * Math.sin(effect.phaseTimer / 1000 * cfg.pulseSpeed * Math.PI * 2)
    : fadeIn;

  // Calculate rectangle corners
  // The line goes from (startX, startY) to (endX, endY)
  // Width is perpendicular to the charge direction
  const halfWidth = (cfg.width * TILE_SIZE) / 2;
  const perpX = Math.cos(effect.angle + Math.PI / 2) * halfWidth;
  const perpY = Math.sin(effect.angle + Math.PI / 2) * halfWidth;

  const sx = effect.startX - camX;
  const sy = effect.startY - camY;
  const ex = effect.endX - camX;
  const ey = effect.endY - camY;

  ctx.save();
  ctx.globalAlpha = cfg.fillOpacity * pulse;

  // Draw filled rectangle as a path (4 corners)
  ctx.fillStyle = cfg.color;
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.moveTo(sx + perpX, sy + perpY);
  ctx.lineTo(ex + perpX, ey + perpY);
  ctx.lineTo(ex - perpX, ey - perpY);
  ctx.lineTo(sx - perpX, sy - perpY);
  ctx.closePath();
  ctx.fill();

  // Draw border
  ctx.globalAlpha = pulse;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.borderColor;
  ctx.lineWidth = cfg.borderWidth;
  ctx.stroke();

  ctx.restore();
},

// ── Draw afterimage trail during dash ──

drawDashTrail(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const trailCfg = effect.config.dash.trail;

  for (let i = 0; i < effect.trailPositions.length; i++) {
    const pos = effect.trailPositions[i];
    const sx = pos.x - camX;
    const sy = pos.y - camY;

    // Each trail image is progressively more transparent
    const trailIndex = effect.trailPositions.length - 1 - i;  // 0 = newest
    const alpha = trailCfg.startAlpha -
      (trailIndex / trailCfg.count) * (trailCfg.startAlpha - trailCfg.endAlpha);

    ctx.save();
    ctx.globalAlpha = alpha;

    // Draw a tinted copy of the enemy sprite
    // Use your existing SpriteRenderer with a tint option
    SpriteRenderer.draw(ctx, effect.enemy.sprite, sx, sy, 2, {
      palette: this.tintPalette(effect.enemy.sprite.palette, trailCfg.tint, 0.4),
    });

    ctx.restore();
  }
},

// ── Updated drawOverlay to handle dash phase ──

drawOverlay(ctx, camX, camY) {
  for (const [id, effect] of EffectAnimator.activeEffects) {
    if (effect.phase === 'dash') {
      this.drawDashTrail(ctx, effect, camX, camY);
      this.drawParticles(ctx, effect, camX, camY);
    }
    if (effect.phase === 'impact') {
      // Choose impact visual based on outcome
      const impactCfg = effect.config.impact[effect.outcome];
      if (impactCfg?.type === 'ring_burst') {
        this.drawImpact(ctx, effect, camX, camY);  // Reuse ground_slam's ring renderer!
      } else if (impactCfg?.type === 'wall_crack') {
        this.drawWallCrack(ctx, effect, camX, camY);
      }
      this.drawParticles(ctx, effect, camX, camY);
    }
  }
},

Part D — The Glue: Connecting to the ability system

Charge needs slightly different hooks than ground_slam because of the dash phase and branching outcomes.

// ─── In enemy-ability-system.js ───
// Modified executeAbility() — detect charge-type abilities

executeAbility(enemy, target, ability, abilitySet) {
  const telegraphTime = /* ... existing calculation ... */;
  const recoveryTime = ability.recoveryTime || 600;

  abilitySet.cooldowns[ability.id] = ability.cooldown;
  abilitySet.globalCooldown = 1000;
  enemy.abilityState = 'telegraph';
  enemy.abilityLockout = true;

  // ── Start the correct effect type ──
  let effectId;
  if (ability.movementType === 'charge') {
    // Charge needs direction + path calculation
    effectId = EffectAnimator.startChargeEffect(
      ability.id, enemy, target.worldX, target.worldY
    );
  } else {
    // Static abilities (ground_slam, stomp, etc.)
    effectId = EffectAnimator.startEffect(
      ability.id, enemy.worldX, enemy.worldY, telegraphTime, ability.radius
    );
  }
  enemy._currentEffectId = effectId;

  // Telegraph timeout — same structure, but resolve triggers dash instead of damage
  const enemyId = enemy.id;
  setTimeout(() => {
    if (!enemy || enemy.hp <= 0) {
      EffectAnimator.cancel(effectId);
      this.cleanupAbilityState(enemy);
      return;
    }
    if (enemy.isStunned || enemy.isRooted) {
      EffectAnimator.cancel(effectId);
      abilitySet.cooldowns[ability.id] = ability.cooldown * 0.5;
      this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime * 0.5, enemyId);
      return;
    }

    if (ability.movementType === 'charge') {
      // ── Start the dash phase ──
      // The EffectAnimator handles movement + collision during dash.
      // It calls back to us when the dash resolves.
      enemy.abilityState = 'resolving';
      EffectAnimator.resolveToDash(effectId, {
        onHitPlayer: (player) => {
          this.applyDamage(player, ability.damage, ability.damageType);
          this.applyStun(player, ability.stunDuration || 1000);
          this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime, enemyId);
        },
        onHitWall: () => {
          this.applyDamage(enemy, 12, 'collision');  // Self-damage
          this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime * 1.8, enemyId);  // Extended recovery
        },
        onMiss: () => {
          this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime, enemyId);
        }
      });
    } else {
      // Static ability (ground_slam path — unchanged)
      EffectAnimator.resolve(effectId);
      this.resolveAbility(enemy, target, ability);
      this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime, enemyId);
    }
  }, telegraphTime);
},
Charge uses callbacks instead of a single resolve() call. With ground_slam, the ability system calls resolve() and immediately applies damage — the outcome is always the same. With charge, the outcome depends on what the Knight hits during the dash, which isn't known until the dash completes. So the ability system passes three callbacks (onHitPlayer, onHitWall, onMiss) and the EffectAnimator calls the right one when the dash ends. This keeps damage/stun application in the ability system (where it belongs) while letting the animation system control movement and collision detection (where that belongs).

6 Adapting This for Other Movement Abilities

Charge is the template for all abilities where the enemy moves. Here's how the same system handles the others:

lunge (Tide Serpent, Frozen Husk)

Same as charge but shorter and faster: 3 tiles instead of 10, 400ms telegraph instead of 800ms, no stun on hit. The data entry is almost identical — change length: 3, width: 1, remove stunEffect, reduce trail count to 2. The entire controller and renderer code is reused without changes.

pounce (Flame Sprite)

Instead of a straight line, pounce follows an arc trajectory. The telegraph is a circle at the landing zone (like ground_slam) rather than a line. During the dash phase, the enemy sprite moves along a parabolic arc (add a jumpHeight property). The controller interpolates position using a quadratic curve instead of linear. On landing, play a small impact ring. This is a hybrid of ground_slam's telegraph + charge's movement.

shadow_step (Shadow Stalker)

No visible movement between positions. The enemy vanishes at point A and appears at point B. Instead of a dash phase with interpolation, use a "vanish" particle burst at the origin (purple smoke), then after 100ms, a "materialize" particle burst at the destination + immediate strike. The 200ms telegraph is the vanish puff itself — by the time the player sees it, the Stalker is already behind them. This is the fastest, most dangerous ability in the game and the animation should feel instantaneous.
