Ability Animation Reference: melee_swing

Complete implementation guide for one ability — covering design intent, sprite frames, JavaScript code, and system integration. Use this as the template for all 22 abilities.

0 What is melee_swing?

Used by: Skeleton Warrior (primary), Ember Knight (primary), Obsidian Sentry (secondary)

Shape: Arc (90° cone), range 1.5 tiles from enemy center, aimed toward target

Damage: 18 base (scaled by tier)

Telegraph: 500ms (half a second — faster than ground_slam, a short windup)

Recovery: 400ms (shorter punish window — melee attacks recover quickly)

Cooldown: 2000ms

Effect: Slash arc visual, metal sparks on impact, slight screen nudge

The player experience: You see the enemy raise its weapon — the arc telegraph flashes briefly on the ground showing the swing's reach. You have half a second to step out of the cone or dodge sideways. If you get hit, sparks fly and the screen nudges. The enemy recovers quickly, so the punish window is tight — you get a couple of hits in before it's ready to swing again.

How melee_swing differs from ground_slam: This is a directional attack, not a radial one. The telegraph is a cone aimed at the player, not a circle centered on the enemy. It's faster (500ms vs 1000ms telegraph) and has a shorter recovery (400ms vs 800ms). This means the player has less warning but also less punishment for getting hit — it's a bread-and-butter attack that enemies use frequently, not a big commitment slam.

1 The Animation Lifecycle

Every ability in the game follows this exact four-phase lifecycle. melee_swing is faster and tighter than ground_slam — the whole cycle is snappier, reflecting a quick melee strike rather than a heavy windup.

TELEGRAPH What the player sees (500ms)

  • A 90° arc appears on the ground, extending 1.5 tiles from the enemy, aimed at the player's position when the swing starts
  • The arc fills in from the inner edge outward (the weapon traces its path)
  • The enemy raises its weapon arm to the high-guard position (weapon up and back)
  • The arc does NOT track — the direction is locked when the telegraph starts
  • A short metallic scraping/ringing sound plays (weapon being drawn back)

This is the warning. The player can sidestep out of the cone — the key counter is to circle around the enemy, not retreat backwards.

RESOLVE What happens on impact (instant)

  • A slash arc line sweeps across the cone in 1–2 frames (the actual blade path)
  • The slash line is bright white with a colored trail matching the weapon type
  • Metal sparks burst from the leading edge of the slash arc
  • A short screen nudge (2px, 100ms) — lighter than ground_slam's full shake
  • A sharp slashing sound plays (metal cutting through air)
  • Damage is applied to all players within the arc

RECOVERY The punish window (400ms)

  • The enemy's weapon arm follows through to a low position (overextended)
  • Brief pause as the enemy resets its stance
  • Enemy takes 1.15x damage during this window (less than ground_slam's 1.25x — melee attacks are less committal)
  • Enemy cannot move or start another ability

A tighter punish window than ground_slam. You get 1–2 quick hits, not a full combo. Players learn to weave attacks between swings.

IDLE Cooldown period (~1100ms)

  • No animation effect — the enemy returns to combat-ready idle stance
  • Enemy repositions to face the player (AI takes over)
  • Cooldown ticks down in the background
  • With a 2000ms total cooldown, the enemy can swing roughly every 2 seconds — this gives a steady, rhythmic combat feel

3 Sprite Frames (What to Draw)

These are the individual frames the animation system draws. For melee_swing, there are three visual components that play at different times. Unlike ground_slam's radial symmetry, these are all directional — they point toward the target.

A. Telegraph Frames (arc indicator — drawn below all entities)

A filling arc on the floor showing the swing's reach. 4 key frames over 500ms. The arc fills from inner edge outward.

B. Impact Frames (the slash effect — drawn above the enemy)

A sweeping slash line that traces the arc path. 5 frames, plays once on resolve. Bright leading edge with a fading trail.

C. Particle Frames (sparks — drawn above everything)

Small bright sparks that burst from the leading edge of the slash. Each spark has a fast outward trajectory with quick falloff. 6–8 sparks, each a 2x2 pixel bright point.

4 JavaScript Implementation

This is the actual code your team writes. It's split into three parts: the data (what the animation looks like), the controller (manages animation state), and the renderer (draws it on screen).

Part A — The Data: Define what melee_swing looks like

This goes in effect-sprites.js alongside ground_slam and all other ability entries.

// ─── effect-sprites.js (add to ABILITY_EFFECTS) ───
// melee_swing: A directional arc attack with a slash visual.
// Key difference from ground_slam: this uses 'arc_fill' instead of 'circle_grow'.
// The telegraph is cone-shaped and aimed at the target.

const ABILITY_EFFECTS = {

  // ... ground_slam (already defined) ...

  melee_swing: {
    // ── TELEGRAPH: arc on the ground showing swing reach ──
    telegraph: {
      type: 'arc_fill',             // Shape type — renderer draws a pie-slice arc
      color: '#FF6B6B',              // Red-tinted fill (danger — melee hit incoming)
      borderColor: '#FF4444',        // Brighter red border edge
      fillOpacity: 0.20,             // Slightly less opaque than ground_slam
      borderWidth: 2,                // Pixel width of the arc border
      startScale: 0.4,               // Starts at 40% of full reach
      endScale: 1.0,                 // Grows to full reach over telegraph duration
      arcAngle: Math.PI / 2,        // 90° cone (π/2 radians)
      radius: 1.5,                   // 1.5 tiles — melee range, MUST match ability hit range
      pulseSpeed: 8,                 // Faster pulse than ground_slam (shorter telegraph)
      directed: true,                // Needs a direction (angle toward target)
      lockDirection: true,           // Direction locks when telegraph starts — does NOT track
    },

    // ── IMPACT: sweeping slash arc ──
    impact: {
      type: 'slash_arc',
      color: '#FFFFFF',              // Bright white leading edge
      trailColor: '#FFCCAA',        // Warm trail behind the slash
      trailFade: 0.6,               // Trail opacity (fades behind the leading edge)
      arcAngle: Math.PI / 2,        // Same 90° as telegraph
      sweepDirection: 'cw',         // Clockwise sweep (top-right to bottom-right)
      radius: 1.6,                   // Slightly past hit range (visual overshoot)
      lineWidth: 3,                  // Width of the slash line
      duration: 150,                 // 150ms — very fast, a quick slash
      fadeStart: 0.5,                // Trail starts fading at 50% of duration
      screenNudge: {                // Lighter than ground_slam's shake
        intensity: 2,                // Max 2px offset (half of ground_slam)
        duration: 100,               // 100ms (half of ground_slam)
        decay: 'linear',
        direction: 'swing',          // Nudge follows the swing direction, not random
      }
    },

    // ── PARTICLES: metal sparks from the slash ──
    particles: {
      count: 7,                      // Fewer than ground_slam's 10 — lighter effect
      sprite: {
        width: 2, height: 2,
        pixels: ['SS', 'SS'],            // Tiny 2x2 bright square
        palette: { 'S': '#FFEECC' }      // Hot white-yellow spark
      },
      spawnMode: 'arc_edge',           // Sparks spawn along the leading edge of the slash
      spawnRadius: 1.5,               // At the slash's reach
      velocity: {
        radialRange: [40, 100],       // Sparks fly outward from slash center
        tangentialRange: [-30, 80],   // Some spread along the arc direction
      },
      gravity: 150,                   // Lighter gravity — sparks float briefly
      lifetime: 350,                  // Short-lived — 350ms (snappy, not lingering)
      fadeOut: 0.4,                   // Start fading at 40% of lifetime
      sizeDecay: true,                // Sparks shrink as they fade
    },

    // ── AUDIO CUES ──
    audio: {
      telegraph: 'sfx_blade_draw',         // Short metallic scrape as weapon is raised
      impact: 'sfx_slash_hit',             // Sharp slicing sound on resolve
    }
  }
};
Key difference from ground_slam: The directed: true and lockDirection: true flags tell the renderer this telegraph needs a target angle. The renderer draws a pie-slice arc instead of a full circle. lockDirection means the direction is captured once when startEffect() is called and doesn't update — this is critical for fairness because the player can dodge sideways after seeing the telegraph.

Part B — The Controller: Manage animation state (additions for directed abilities)

The existing EffectAnimator from ground_slam handles most of this. melee_swing adds a direction parameter and a different particle spawn mode.

// ─── effect-animator.js (modified startEffect for directed abilities) ───

startEffect(abilityId, x, y, telegraphMs, radius, /* NEW → */ direction) {
  const id = this.nextId++;
  const config = ABILITY_EFFECTS[abilityId];

  if (!config) {
    console.warn(`No effect defined for ability: ${abilityId}`);
    return -1;
  }

  this.activeEffects.set(id, {
    id,
    abilityId,
    config,
    x, y,
    radius,

    // ── Phase tracking ──
    phase: 'telegraph',
    phaseTimer: 0,
    telegraphDuration: telegraphMs,

    // ════ NEW: Direction for aimed abilities ════
    // For ground_slam this is undefined (radial).
    // For melee_swing this is the angle from enemy → target in radians.
    direction: direction || 0,

    // ── Particle state ──
    particles: [],
  });

  return id;
},

// ── Modified spawnParticles for 'arc_edge' mode ──

spawnParticles(effect) {
  const cfg = effect.config.particles;
  if (!cfg) return;

  if (cfg.spawnMode === 'arc_edge') {
    // Sparks spawn along the arc's outer edge
    const arcAngle = effect.config.telegraph.arcAngle;
    const startAngle = effect.direction - arcAngle / 2;

    for (let i = 0; i < cfg.count; i++) {
      const angle = startAngle + Math.random() * arcAngle;
      const dist = cfg.spawnRadius * TILE_SIZE * (0.8 + Math.random() * 0.2);

      // Radial + tangential velocity
      const radialSpeed = cfg.velocity.radialRange[0]
        + Math.random() * (cfg.velocity.radialRange[1] - cfg.velocity.radialRange[0]);
      const tangentSpeed = cfg.velocity.tangentialRange[0]
        + Math.random() * (cfg.velocity.tangentialRange[1] - cfg.velocity.tangentialRange[0]);

      effect.particles.push({
        x: effect.x + Math.cos(angle) * dist,
        y: effect.y + Math.sin(angle) * dist,
        vx: Math.cos(angle) * radialSpeed + Math.cos(angle + Math.PI/2) * tangentSpeed,
        vy: Math.sin(angle) * radialSpeed + Math.sin(angle + Math.PI/2) * tangentSpeed,
        life: cfg.lifetime,
        maxLife: cfg.lifetime,
        sprite: cfg.sprite,
      });
    }
  } else {
    // Original radial spawn for ground_slam-style abilities
    // ... (existing code unchanged) ...
  }
}
Critical — direction parameter: When the ability system calls startEffect(), it must compute the angle from the enemy to the target at that moment: Math.atan2(target.y - enemy.y, target.x - enemy.x). This angle is stored once and never updated. If you accidentally update it during the telegraph, the arc will track the player and become impossible to dodge — this destroys the fairness contract.

Part C — The Renderer: Draw the arc telegraph and slash

New rendering functions for arc_fill telegraphs and slash_arc impact visuals. These supplement (not replace) the existing circle renderers.

// ─── effect-renderer.js (add to EffectRenderer) ───

// ── TELEGRAPH: Filling arc on the ground ──
drawTelegraph(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.telegraph;

  // Dispatch to the right shape renderer
  if (cfg.type === 'circle_grow') {
    this.drawTelegraphCircle(ctx, effect, camX, camY);   // existing
    return;
  }

  if (cfg.type === 'arc_fill') {
    this.drawTelegraphArc(ctx, effect, camX, camY);      // NEW
    return;
  }
},

drawTelegraphArc(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.telegraph;

  // How far through the telegraph? 0.0 → 1.0
  const progress = Math.min(effect.phaseTimer / effect.telegraphDuration, 1.0);

  // Current radius: lerp from startScale to endScale
  const scale = cfg.startScale + (cfg.endScale - cfg.startScale) * progress;
  const radiusPx = cfg.radius * TILE_SIZE * scale;

  // Screen position
  const sx = effect.x - camX;
  const sy = effect.y - camY;

  // Arc angles: centered on effect.direction, spanning cfg.arcAngle
  const halfArc = cfg.arcAngle / 2;
  const startAngle = effect.direction - halfArc;
  const endAngle = effect.direction + halfArc;

  // Pulsing border
  const pulse = 0.5 + 0.5 * Math.sin(effect.phaseTimer / 1000 * cfg.pulseSpeed * Math.PI * 2);
  const borderAlpha = 0.5 + pulse * 0.5;

  // Draw filled arc (pie slice)
  ctx.save();
  ctx.globalAlpha = cfg.fillOpacity;
  ctx.fillStyle = cfg.color;
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.moveTo(sx, sy);                               // Start at center
  ctx.arc(sx, sy, radiusPx, startAngle, endAngle);   // Arc to outer edge
  ctx.closePath();                                   // Line back to center
  ctx.fill();

  // Draw border
  ctx.globalAlpha = borderAlpha;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.borderColor;
  ctx.lineWidth = cfg.borderWidth;
  ctx.stroke();
  ctx.restore();
},

// ── IMPACT: Sweeping slash line ──
drawImpact(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.impact;

  if (cfg.type === 'ring_burst') {
    this.drawImpactRings(ctx, effect, camX, camY);    // existing
    return;
  }

  if (cfg.type === 'slash_arc') {
    this.drawSlashArc(ctx, effect, camX, camY);       // NEW
    return;
  }
},

drawSlashArc(ctx, effect, camX, camY) {
  const cfg = effect.config.impact;
  const progress = Math.min(effect.phaseTimer / cfg.duration, 1.0);

  const sx = effect.x - camX;
  const sy = effect.y - camY;
  const radiusPx = cfg.radius * TILE_SIZE;

  // The slash sweeps across the arc over the duration
  const halfArc = cfg.arcAngle / 2;
  const arcStart = effect.direction - halfArc;
  const arcEnd = effect.direction + halfArc;

  // Leading edge position (sweeps from arcStart → arcEnd)
  const leadAngle = arcStart + (arcEnd - arcStart) * progress;

  // Trail: draw a thick arc from start up to the leading edge
  const trailStart = Math.max(arcStart, leadAngle - halfArc * 0.6);

  // Fade based on progress
  const alpha = progress > cfg.fadeStart
    ? 1.0 - ((progress - cfg.fadeStart) / (1.0 - cfg.fadeStart))
    : 1.0;

  ctx.save();

  // Draw trail (faded, behind leading edge)
  ctx.globalAlpha = alpha * cfg.trailFade;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.trailColor;
  ctx.lineWidth = cfg.lineWidth + 1;
  ctx.lineCap = 'round';
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.arc(sx, sy, radiusPx, trailStart, leadAngle);
  ctx.stroke();

  // Draw leading edge (bright white, sharper)
  ctx.globalAlpha = alpha;
  ctx.strokeStyle = cfg.color;
  ctx.lineWidth = cfg.lineWidth;
  const edgeSpan = 0.15;  // Leading edge covers 15% of the arc
  ctx.beginPath();
  ctx.arc(sx, sy, radiusPx, leadAngle - edgeSpan, leadAngle);
  ctx.stroke();

  ctx.restore();
}
Why a sweeping slash instead of an instant flash? A single-frame flash would feel like a "pop" — the player wouldn't read it as a blade moving through space. By sweeping the slash line across the arc over 150ms, the player's eye tracks the motion and reads it as "a weapon just swung through here." The bright leading edge is the blade tip; the faded trail is the afterimage. This is the same technique used in games like Hades and Dead Cells to sell melee impacts.

Part D — The Glue: Connect it to the ability system

The only change from ground_slam's glue code is passing the direction parameter when starting the effect.

// ─── In enemy-ability-system.js ───
// Modified executeAbility() — for directed abilities, compute and pass angle

executeAbility(enemy, target, ability, abilitySet) {
  const telegraphTime = /* ... existing telegraph calculation ... */;
  const recoveryTime = ability.recoveryTime || 600;

  // ════ NEW: Compute direction for aimed abilities ════
  // Capture the angle NOW — this is the direction the swing will face.
  // For non-directed abilities (ground_slam), direction is undefined/ignored.
  let direction = undefined;
  const effectConfig = ABILITY_EFFECTS[ability.id];
  if (effectConfig?.telegraph?.directed) {
    direction = Math.atan2(
      target.worldY - enemy.worldY,
      target.worldX - enemy.worldX
    );
  }

  // Existing: set cooldowns, lock enemy state...
  abilitySet.cooldowns[ability.id] = ability.cooldown;
  abilitySet.globalCooldown = 1000;
  enemy.abilityState = 'telegraph';
  enemy.abilityLockout = true;

  // Start the visual effect (now with direction)
  const effectId = EffectAnimator.startEffect(
    ability.id,
    enemy.worldX, enemy.worldY,
    telegraphTime,
    ability.radius || 0,
    direction                                   // ← NEW param (undefined for radial)
  );

  enemy._currentEffectId = effectId;

  // ════ NEW: Face the enemy toward the swing direction ════
  // So the enemy sprite visually matches the telegraph direction.
  if (direction !== undefined) {
    enemy.facingAngle = direction;
  }

  // ... rest is identical to ground_slam (setTimeout, resolve, etc.) ...
}
Critical: The direction is computed ONCE in executeAbility() and passed into the effect. During the 500ms telegraph, the enemy does NOT rotate to track the player. The enemy's facingAngle is set at the start and stays locked. If you update facingAngle during the telegraph, both the enemy sprite and the telegraph arc will desync — the arc stays aimed at the original position but the enemy visually turns, which looks broken.

5 How the Render Loop Calls It

No changes needed to the render loop from ground_slam. The existing two-pass system (drawGround + drawOverlay) handles melee_swing automatically because the renderer dispatches based on type — the new arc_fill and slash_arc types just plug in.

// ─── In your main game loop (unchanged from ground_slam setup) ───

update(deltaTime) {
  // ... existing game update code ...
  EnemyAbilitySystem.update(deltaTime);
  EffectAnimator.update(deltaTime);          // Handles both circle and arc effects
}

render(ctx) {
  TileRenderer.draw(ctx);                      // 1. Floor tiles
  EffectRenderer.drawGround(ctx, camera.x, camera.y);  // 2. Telegraphs (circles AND arcs)
  EntityRenderer.draw(ctx);                    // 3. Entities
  EffectRenderer.drawOverlay(ctx, camera.x, camera.y);  // 4. Impacts (rings AND slashes)
  UIRenderer.draw(ctx);                        // 5. UI
}
This is the power of the data-driven approach. Because ground_slam already established the rendering pipeline, adding melee_swing required zero changes to the main loop. The new arc_fill and slash_arc renderer functions just plug into the existing dispatch. Every future ability — cone, line, projectile — works the same way: define the data, add a renderer for the shape type, done.

6 Adapting This for Other Melee Variants

melee_swing is the base case for all close-range weapon attacks. The same arc_fill telegraph and slash_arc impact work for variants with different feel:

For heavy_cleave (two-handed axe, slower and wider):

Same arc_fill type but wider arc (120° instead of 90°), longer range (2.0 tiles), slower telegraph (700ms), and heavier impact. Increase particle count to 12 and add a brief screen shake instead of a nudge. Recovery is longer (600ms) — bigger punish window for a bigger commitment. Change slash color to a darker orange for a "heavy iron" feel.

For quick_stab (dagger, fast and narrow):

Narrow arc (45°), short range (1.0 tile), very fast telegraph (300ms). Impact type changes to thrust_line — a single line extending from the enemy center rather than a sweeping arc. Fewer sparks (3–4), smaller and faster. Almost no recovery (200ms). This attack is about volume, not damage — the enemy stabs often and the player weaves between strikes.

For spin_attack (360° melee sweep):

Change arcAngle to Math.PI * 2 (full circle) and the telegraph becomes a full ring — visually similar to ground_slam's circle but the impact is a slash_arc that sweeps 360° instead of ring_burst. The slash spins around the enemy. This bridges melee_swing and ground_slam — it's a melee attack with area-denial properties. Longer telegraph (800ms) since there's no safe angle.

For combo_strike (2-hit or 3-hit chain):

Multiple melee_swings fired in rapid sequence with alternating directions. The first swing goes left-to-right (CW), the second right-to-left (CCW), optional third is an overhead slam (small circle). Each has its own telegraph/resolve cycle but with very short gaps (100ms) between them. The ability system queues them as separate effects. The chain can be interrupted between swings but not during a swing.
