Ability Animation Reference: homing_orb

Pyro Cultist signature ability — a slow-tracking projectile that lives independently in the world. This covers mechanics that ground_slam and charge didn't: independent projectile entities, homing behavior, caster-based telegraphs, and projectiles that outlive their caster.

0 What is homing_orb?

Used by: Pyro Cultist (primary)

Shape: Single projectile, approximately 1 tile diameter visual

Damage: 25 base (scaled by tier)

Telegraph: 800ms — the Cultist gathers energy in its hands (visual on the caster, NOT on the ground)

Travel speed: 4 tiles/sec — slower than walking speed (~5 tiles/sec). The player can outrun it.

Tracking: Turns toward the nearest player at ~90°/sec — it curves, it doesn't snap

Lifetime: 10 seconds — the orb despawns if it hasn't hit anything by then

Recovery: 500ms — ranged enemies shouldn't be safe just because they're far away

Cooldown: 8000ms

Persists after caster death: Yes — killing the Cultist does NOT remove orbs already in flight

The player experience: The Cultist raises its hands and a glowing orb forms — you have 800ms to prepare. Then the orb launches and slowly drifts toward you. You can outrun it, but you can't ignore it. It follows you around corners, forces you to keep moving, and splits your attention between the orb and the enemies in front of you. If you lead it into a wall or another enemy, it dies. It's a slow, persistent threat that constrains your positioning for up to 10 seconds.

The fundamental difference: ground_slam and charge are effects — they happen, they resolve, they're gone. Homing orb creates an entity. Once launched, the orb is a fully independent object in the game world with its own position, velocity, collision, and lifetime. It needs its own update loop, its own rendering, and its own cleanup. This is architecturally closer to spawning a new enemy than playing an animation.

1 The Animation Lifecycle

Homing orb's timeline is fundamentally different because the travel phase is variable-length. It could last 500ms (if the player is right in front of the Cultist) or the full 10 seconds (if the player keeps running). The other abilities have fixed timelines — the animation system knows how long they'll last when they start. Homing orb doesn't.

The caster's lifecycle and the orb's lifecycle are SEPARATE. The Cultist enters telegraph → recovery → idle as a fixed cycle (just like any other enemy). The orb, once spawned, has its own travel → impact lifecycle that runs completely independently. The Cultist could die, and the orb keeps going. The Cultist could fire a second orb (if the first is still alive) after 8 seconds. The animation system must track these as two separate things: an enemy ability state (on the Cultist) and a projectile entity (in the world).

TELEGRAPH Caster windup (800ms)

  • The Cultist raises both hands — energy gathers between them
  • A glowing orb grows above the Cultist's hands, starting small and reaching full size
  • The orb pulses and crackles with orange sparks during formation
  • A rising hum sound builds for 800ms
  • No ground telegraph — the warning is on the caster, not the floor
  • This communicates: "a projectile is coming" not "this area is dangerous"

The player reads the telegraph and knows an orb is about to launch. They can interrupt the Cultist during this window to cancel the orb (stun/knockback), or they can prepare to dodge the launch direction.

TRAVEL Orb in flight (variable)

  • The orb launches from the Cultist toward the target's current position
  • It moves at 4 tiles/sec — visibly slower than the player
  • It turns toward the nearest player at ~90°/sec — smooth curves, not instant snapping
  • A trailing particle stream follows behind (orange embers fading to red)
  • The orb itself pulses (scale oscillates between 0.9 and 1.1) and rotates slowly
  • A quiet hum/crackle sound loops while the orb is alive, with volume based on distance to player

The orb is a persistent pressure tool. It doesn't kill you — it constrains your movement. You're dodging other enemies while also keeping track of a slow orb drifting toward you from behind.

IMPACT Three possible endings

A — Hit player:

  • 25 damage applied
  • Explosion burst — expanding fire ring (reuses ring_burst renderer)
  • Screen shake (2px, 100ms)
  • Fire impact sound

B — Hit wall/obstacle:

  • No damage — the orb just dies
  • Smaller fizzle burst — sparks scatter, no ring
  • Quiet pop sound

C — Timeout (10 seconds):

  • The orb flickers rapidly for the last 500ms of its life
  • Then fades out with a small spark shower
  • No damage, quiet fizzle sound

RECOVERY On the caster, not the orb (500ms)

  • The Cultist's recovery starts the moment the orb launches
  • It does NOT wait for the orb to hit something
  • Standard recovery: stagger animation, 1.25x damage taken, no movement
  • After recovery, the Cultist resumes kiting behavior

The orb is gone from the Cultist's perspective. The Cultist moves on. The orb is the world's problem now.

Key timing detail:

The cooldown (8000ms) starts at telegraph, same as every other ability. Effective idle time: 8000ms - 800ms (telegraph) - 500ms (recovery) = 6700ms before the next orb. In practice, the first orb is often still alive when the second one launches. Multiple orbs are expected. The system must support it.

2 Key Differences from ground_slam and charge

Mechanicground_slam / chargehoming_orbWhat changes
Telegraph visual Ground indicator (circle/line) Caster-attached visual No drawGround() call. Instead, draw a glowing orb above the caster's sprite during windup. The telegraph renderer reads enemy.abilityState === 'telegraph' and draws at the enemy's position.
What gets created Temporary visual effect Independent world entity The orb needs: a position, velocity, facing angle, lifetime counter, collision radius, and its own update/render cycle. This is a Projectile object stored in a global projectile list, not an entry in EffectAnimator.
Duration Fixed (telegraph + recovery) Variable (up to 10 seconds) No setTimeout for the orb's lifecycle. It runs in the game loop until it hits something, hits a wall, or times out.
Movement None / linear dash Homing (curves toward target) Each frame: calculate angle to target, rotate current heading toward that angle by turnRate * dt, move forward at speed * dt. This creates smooth curves, not instant tracking.
Caster dies Effect cancels Orb persists The projectile has no reference to the caster after launch. Caster death does NOT trigger cancel(). The orb is fully decoupled.

Rendering layer Ground (telegraph) + overlay (impact) Entity layer (same as monsters/players) The orb is drawn in the entity pass, Y-sorted with monsters and players so it can appear behind or in front of other entities naturally.
Collision AoE check at fixed time / per-frame during dash Circle-vs-circle per frame, continuous Each frame: check orb's collision radius against all players AND all wall tiles. First hit wins. Simple distance check: dist(orb, player) < orb.radius + player.radius.

Part B — The Projectile System: A new system, not part of EffectAnimator

This is the key architectural decision. Homing orb does NOT go into EffectAnimator. The telegraph does (it's a temporary visual on the caster). But the orb itself goes into a new ProjectileSystem that manages all in-flight projectiles. This system also handles web_shot, projectile_burst, projectile_single, and projectile_volley — every ability that creates a traveling object. If you try to jam projectiles into EffectAnimator, you'll end up fighting its fixed-timeline assumptions. 
// ─── NEW FILE: js/systems/projectile-system.js ───
// Manages ALL in-flight projectiles as independent entities.

const ProjectileSystem = {
  projectiles: [],   // Active projectile entities
  nextId: 0,

  /**
   * Spawn a new projectile into the world.
   * Called when an ability with a projectile resolves (after telegraph).
   *
   * @param config     - The projectile config from ABILITY_EFFECTS
   * @param x, y       - World position where the projectile spawns
   * @param targetX, Y - Initial target position (for launch direction)
   * @param damage     - How much damage on hit
   * @param effects    - Status effects to apply on hit (optional)
   * @returns projectile id
   */
  spawn(config, x, y, targetX, targetY, damage, effects) {
    const angle = Math.atan2(targetY - y, targetX - x);

    const proj = {
      id: this.nextId++,
      config,
      damage,
      effects: effects || [],

      // ── Position & movement ──
      x, y,
      angle,                             // Current heading (radians)
      speed: config.speed * TILE_SIZE,    // Convert tiles/sec to pixels/sec
      turnRate: (config.turnRate || 0) * (Math.PI / 180),  // Convert deg/sec to rad/sec

      // ── Collision ──
      collisionRadius: (config.collisionRadius || 0.4) * TILE_SIZE,

      // ── Lifecycle ──
      age: 0,
      lifetime: config.lifetime || 10000,
      alive: true,

      // ── Visual state ──
      trailParticles: [],
      trailSpawnAccumulator: 0,

      // ── Audio ──
      travelSoundId: null,
    };

    this.projectiles.push(proj);
    return proj.id;
  },

  /**
   * Called every frame by the game loop.
   * Updates ALL in-flight projectiles.
   */
  update(deltaTime, players, walls) {
    for (const proj of this.projectiles) {
      if (!proj.alive) continue;

      proj.age += deltaTime;

      // ── HOMING: curve toward nearest player ──
      if (proj.turnRate > 0) {
        const target = this.findNearestPlayer(proj, players);
        if (target) {
          const desiredAngle = Math.atan2(
            target.worldY - proj.y,
            target.worldX - proj.x
          );

          // Rotate toward target at limited turn rate
          let angleDiff = desiredAngle - proj.angle;

          // Normalize to [-PI, PI] so it takes the shorter turn
          while (angleDiff > Math.PI) angleDiff -= Math.PI * 2;
          while (angleDiff < -Math.PI) angleDiff += Math.PI * 2;

          const maxTurn = proj.turnRate * (deltaTime / 1000);
          proj.angle += Math.sign(angleDiff) * Math.min(Math.abs(angleDiff), maxTurn);
        }
      }

      // ── MOVE: advance along current heading ──
      proj.x += Math.cos(proj.angle) * proj.speed * (deltaTime / 1000);
      proj.y += Math.sin(proj.angle) * proj.speed * (deltaTime / 1000);

      // ── COLLISION: check players ──
      for (const player of players) {
        const dist = Math.hypot(proj.x - player.worldX, proj.y - player.worldY);
        if (dist < proj.collisionRadius + player.collisionRadius) {
          this.resolveHit(proj, 'hitPlayer', player);
          return;  // Orb is consumed
        }
      }

      // ── COLLISION: check walls ──
      if (this.isInWall(proj.x, proj.y, walls)) {
        this.resolveHit(proj, 'hitWall', null);
        return;
      }

      // ── TIMEOUT ──
      if (proj.age >= proj.lifetime) {
        this.resolveHit(proj, 'timeout', null);
        return;
      }

      // ── TRAIL PARTICLES ──
      this.updateTrail(proj, deltaTime);
    }

    // Remove dead projectiles
    this.projectiles = this.projectiles.filter(p => p.alive);
  },

  /**
   * Projectile hit something (or timed out).
   * Apply damage, spawn impact effect, kill the projectile.
   */
  resolveHit(proj, outcome, target) {
    proj.alive = false;

    // Apply damage if hitting a player
    if (outcome === 'hitPlayer' && target) {
      EnemyAbilitySystem.applyDamage(target, proj.damage, 'fire');
      for (const effect of proj.effects) {
        EnemyAbilitySystem.applyStatusEffect(target, effect);
      }
    }

    // Spawn impact visual (using EffectAnimator — impacts ARE temporary effects)
    const impactConfig = ABILITY_EFFECTS.homing_orb.impact[outcome];
    if (impactConfig) {
      EffectAnimator.startImpactEffect(impactConfig, proj.x, proj.y);
    }

    // Play impact audio
    const audioKey = outcome === 'hitPlayer' ? 'impactPlayer'
      : outcome === 'hitWall' ? 'impactWall' : 'timeout';
    AudioManager.play(ABILITY_EFFECTS.homing_orb.audio[audioKey]);

    // Stop travel sound
    if (proj.travelSoundId) {
      AudioManager.stop(proj.travelSoundId);
    }
  },

  // ── Trail particle management ──

  updateTrail(proj, deltaTime) {
    const trailCfg = proj.config.trail;
    if (!trailCfg) return;

    // Spawn new trail particles at the configured rate
    proj.trailSpawnAccumulator += deltaTime;
    const spawnInterval = 1000 / trailCfg.spawnRate;

    while (proj.trailSpawnAccumulator >= spawnInterval) {
      proj.trailSpawnAccumulator -= spawnInterval;
      proj.trailParticles.push({
        x: proj.x,
        y: proj.y,
        vx: trailCfg.velocity.xRange[0] + Math.random() * (trailCfg.velocity.xRange[1] - trailCfg.velocity.xRange[0]),
        vy: trailCfg.velocity.yRange[0] + Math.random() * (trailCfg.velocity.yRange[1] - trailCfg.velocity.yRange[0]),
        life: trailCfg.lifetime,
        maxLife: trailCfg.lifetime,
      });
    }

    // Update existing trail particles
    for (const p of proj.trailParticles) {
      p.x += p.vx * (deltaTime / 1000);
      p.y += p.vy * (deltaTime / 1000);
      p.life -= deltaTime;
    }
    proj.trailParticles = proj.trailParticles.filter(p => p.life > 0);
  },

  findNearestPlayer(proj, players) {
    let nearest = null, nearestDist = Infinity;
    for (const p of players) {
      if (p.hp <= 0) continue;
      const d = Math.hypot(proj.x - p.worldX, proj.y - p.worldY);
      if (d < nearestDist) { nearest = p; nearestDist = d; }
    }
    return nearest;
  },
};

Part C — The Renderer: Drawing the orb and trail

// ─── In effect-renderer.js OR a new projectile-renderer.js ───
// Projectiles render in the ENTITY layer (Y-sorted with monsters and players)

const ProjectileRenderer = {

  drawAll(ctx, camX, camY) {
    for (const proj of ProjectileSystem.projectiles) {
      if (!proj.alive) continue;
      this.drawTrail(ctx, proj, camX, camY);
      this.drawOrb(ctx, proj, camX, camY);
    }
  },

  drawOrb(ctx, proj, camX, camY) {
    const sx = proj.x - camX;
    const sy = proj.y - camY;
    const cfg = proj.config;

    // Pulse scale
    const pulseT = Math.sin(proj.age / 1000 * cfg.pulse.speed * Math.PI * 2);
    const scale = cfg.pulse.scaleMin + (cfg.pulse.scaleMax - cfg.pulse.scaleMin) * (pulseT * 0.5 + 0.5);

    // Flicker when near timeout
    let alpha = 1.0;
    if (proj.age > cfg.flickerStart) {
      alpha = Math.sin(proj.age / 40) * 0.5 + 0.5;  // Rapid flicker
    }

    ctx.save();
    ctx.globalAlpha = alpha;

    // Outer glow (additive-style circle behind the sprite)
    const glowRadius = 10 * scale;
    const gradient = ctx.createRadialGradient(sx, sy, 0, sx, sy, glowRadius);
    gradient.addColorStop(0, 'rgba(255, 170, 0, 0.4)');
    gradient.addColorStop(1, 'rgba(255, 107, 53, 0)');
    ctx.fillStyle = gradient;
    ctx.beginPath();
    ctx.arc(sx, sy, glowRadius, 0, Math.PI * 2);
    ctx.fill();

    // Draw the pixel sprite (scaled and centered)
    EffectRenderer.drawPixelSprite(ctx, cfg.sprite, sx, sy, 2 * scale);

    ctx.restore();
  },

  drawTrail(ctx, proj, camX, camY) {
    const trailCfg = proj.config.trail;
    if (!trailCfg) return;

    for (const p of proj.trailParticles) {
      const lifeRatio = p.life / p.maxLife;
      const alpha = lifeRatio < trailCfg.fadeOut
        ? lifeRatio / trailCfg.fadeOut
        : 1.0;
      ctx.save();
      ctx.globalAlpha = alpha * 0.7;
      ctx.fillStyle = trailCfg.sprite.palette['E'];
      ctx.fillRect(p.x - camX - 1, p.y - camY - 1, 3, 3);
      ctx.restore();
    }
  },
};

Part D — The Glue: Connecting to the ability system

// ─── In enemy-ability-system.js ───
// For projectile abilities, the telegraph works the same.
// On resolve, spawn a projectile instead of dealing direct damage.

executeAbility(enemy, target, ability, abilitySet) {
  const telegraphTime = /* ... existing ... */;
  const recoveryTime = ability.recoveryTime || 600;

  abilitySet.cooldowns[ability.id] = ability.cooldown;
  abilitySet.globalCooldown = 1000;
  enemy.abilityState = 'telegraph';
  enemy.abilityLockout = true;

  // Telegraph — use caster_charge type (visual on enemy, not ground)
  const effectId = EffectAnimator.startCasterEffect(ability.id, enemy, telegraphTime);
  enemy._currentEffectId = effectId;

  const enemyId = enemy.id;
  setTimeout(() => {
    if (!enemy || enemy.hp <= 0) {
      EffectAnimator.cancel(effectId);
      this.cleanupAbilityState(enemy);
      return;
    }
    if (enemy.isStunned || enemy.isRooted) {
      EffectAnimator.cancel(effectId);  // Telegraph vanishes — no orb spawns
      abilitySet.cooldowns[ability.id] = ability.cooldown * 0.5;
      this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime * 0.5, enemyId);
      return;
    }

    // ════ SPAWN PROJECTILE ════
    // The orb becomes an independent entity in the world.
    // From this point, the Cultist and the orb are DECOUPLED.
    EffectAnimator.cancel(effectId);  // Remove caster telegraph visual

    const projConfig = ABILITY_EFFECTS[ability.id].projectile;
    ProjectileSystem.spawn(
      projConfig,
      enemy.worldX,
      enemy.worldY + ABILITY_EFFECTS[ability.id].telegraph.offsetY,  // Launch from above hands
      target.worldX,
      target.worldY,
      ability.damage,
      ability.effects || []
    );

    AudioManager.play(ABILITY_EFFECTS[ability.id].audio.launch);

    // Caster enters recovery immediately — doesn't wait for orb
    this.enterRecovery(enemy, recoveryTime, enemyId);

  }, telegraphTime);
},
Notice the clean separation. The ability system handles: telegraph timing, interrupts, cooldowns, recovery. The ProjectileSystem handles: movement, homing, collision, lifetime. The EffectAnimator handles: temporary visuals (telegraph glow, impact rings). Each system does one job. The orb doesn't know who launched it. The Cultist doesn't know where the orb is. They communicate through events, not shared state.

7 Adapting This for Other Projectile Abilities

web_shot (Crystal Spider)

Same spawn pattern, but: no homing (turnRate: 0 — travels in a straight line), faster speed (8 tiles/sec), shorter lifetime (3s), and applies root instead of damage on hit. The projectile sprite is white/gray web ball. Impact visual is a web net expanding at the hit point. Straight-line projectiles are just homing orbs with turnRate: 0.

projectile_burst (Cinder Wisp)

Three projectiles spawned simultaneously at different angles: -15°, 0°, +15° from the target direction. Each is an independent projectile with turnRate: 0. Call ProjectileSystem.spawn() three times with different targetX/targetY offsets. The system handles the rest — three separate collision checks, three separate impacts.

projectile_volley (Pyro Cultist secondary — if dual abilities are adopted)

Eight projectiles in a 360° ring, all non-homing. Spawn 8 projectiles at 45° intervals. Pure "get away from me" panic ability. Same ProjectileSystem, just spawn() in a loop.

projectile_single (Blizzard Spirit)

The simplest case: one straight-line projectile, no homing, fast speed (8 tiles/sec), short lifetime. This is a web_shot with different damage and no root effect. Literally change the sprite palette and damage value.
